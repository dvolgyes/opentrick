# 3NT with 25–27 combined HCP

The production browser card engine played 12 complete boards against itself.
It made 3NT on nine boards, including all seven boards that were makeable against perfect defense.
Two additional makes depended on defensive errors.
These are results from a small fixed-deal sample, rather than an estimate of the general make rate.

| North–South HCP | Boards | Made 3NT | Makeable against perfect defense | Actual declarer tricks | Double-dummy declarer tricks |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 25 | 4 | 2 | 1 | 8, 11, 8, 9 | 7, 8, 8, 9 |
| 26 | 4 | 3 | 2 | 8, 9, 10, 11 | 8, 9, 5, 9 |
| 27 | 4 | 4 | 4 | 11, 11, 12, 10 | 10, 10, 10, 9 |

## Protocol fixed before play

The generator took the first four qualifying deals for each of three fixed seeds: 20261025, 20261026, and 20261027.
North had 15–17 HCP and a balanced 4333, 4432, or 5332 shape.
North–South had exactly 25, 26, or 27 HCP.
There was no selection on suit stoppers, fit, double-dummy makeability, or observed play.
Some deals therefore have better suit contracts than 3NT.

North declared a fixed, legal 3NT contract after a 1NT opening and either a direct raise or an invitational 2NT response followed by acceptance.
The engine received the public auction meanings.
Bidding choice was outside the test.
All seats used the production `BrowserPlayEngine`, its default budgets, and its usual hidden-information restrictions.
Both declarer's hands and both defenders were played to the end.

The engine was not changed or tuned on these deals.
The separate double-dummy evaluation used the actual hidden hands only for measurement.
It computed the declarer's trick count against optimal defense from the opening lead and evaluated each played card afterward.

## Verification and limits

All 624 cards were legal and all 12 boards ended with 13 tricks.
The results reconcile exactly: initial double-dummy tricks plus defenders' lost tricks minus declarer's lost tricks equal the actual final trick count on every board.
An independent HCP tally confirmed each partnership's point total.
The browser made zero OpenRouter requests.
The longest observed card decision took 1,500.3 ms, including the browser worker call.

Across all boards, declarer's choices lost four tricks relative to perfect-information choices; defenders' choices lost twenty.
Those differences can arise from hidden information as well as poor choices.
In particular, success against this defense does not establish success against perfect defense.
Passing all seven initially makeable contracts does not show that declarer always chose optimally after later defensive errors.

One 26-HCP deal illustrates why points alone do not guarantee 3NT.
The partnership had nine spades together but only four low clubs; West held seven clubs headed by ace, king, and queen.
Perfect defense holds 3NT to five tricks, while the actual defense let declarer take ten.
The fixed contract deliberately left that deal in the sample.

Run environment: engine commit `11097a6`, Node 22.22.1, Google Chrome 156.0.8078.47, Linux x64.
Deal generation is reproducible; the production worker's Monte Carlo search remains stochastic, so later play traces can differ.
The [raw results](3nt-25-27.jsonl) include the protocol, complete deals, auctions, all 52 choices per board, sample counts, timings, and summary.

```sh
npm run --silent benchmark:3nt
```

The command starts and closes its own local Vite server and headless Chrome instance.
It requires no API key and makes no paid requests.

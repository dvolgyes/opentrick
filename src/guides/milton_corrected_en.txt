# Bidding guide: corrected Milton HCP
Count A=4, K=3, Q=2, J=1, then add the corrections:
- −1 for a singleton K, Q or J.
- −1 for the doubletons AJ, KQ, KJ and QJ, and for a jack with one lower card.
- −1 for a 4333 shape.
- +1 for all four aces.
- +1 for each suit of five or more cards with three of A K Q J 10.
- In the shapes 4441, 5440, 5422, 5431, 5521 and 5530: +1 for a doubleton, +2 for a singleton, +3 for a void.
The corrections already include the distribution, so do not add length points. The point ranges are the same as for plain HCP.
This guide uses a natural five-card-major method, similar to Standard American.

## Opening bids
- 0–11 points: [P], unless a weak two or a preempt fits.
- 5–11 points: weak two, [2D] [2H] [2S]. A six-card suit with two of A K Q or three of A K Q J 10, and no four-card side major.
- 5–10 points: preempt, [3C] [3D] [3H] [3S]. A seven-card suit with the same top cards as a weak two.
- 12–21 points: [1C] [1D] [1H] [1S]. [1H] or [1S] needs five or more cards in the suit; with two five-card suits, open the higher. Without a five-card major, open the longer minor: [1D] with 4–4 in the minors, [1C] with 3–3.
- 15–17 points: [1NT], balanced: at least two cards in each suit and at most one doubleton (4333, 4432, 5332).
- 20–21 points: [2NT], balanced as for [1NT].
- 22 or more points: [2C], any shape. Artificial and forcing. With a balanced 22–24 points, rebid [2NT] after partner's answer.
- 25–27 points: [3NT], balanced as for [1NT], instead of [2C].

## Responses to one of a suit
- 0–5 points: [P].
- 6–9 points: raise partner's major one level ([1H] – [2H]) with three or more cards. Otherwise bid a new suit at the one level with four or more cards, or [1NT].
- 10–12 points: invitational. Jump raise partner's major ([1S] – [3S]) with three or more cards, or bid a new suit. A new suit at the two level needs 10 or more points.
- 13 or more points: game forcing. Bid a new suit, and continue until game ([3NT], [4H], [4S]).

## Opener's rebid
- 12–14 points: [1NT] with a balanced hand, a single raise, or a rebid of your own suit.
- 15–17 points: a jump raise, or a jump rebid of your own suit.
- 18–21 points: a jump in a new suit, or [2NT] with a balanced 18–19 points.
- After a single raise of your major ([1H] – [2H]) and a pass: [P] with 12–14 points; [3H] with 15–17 points is a game try and invites game; [4H] with 18–21 points.
- If an opponent bids or doubles after the raise: [3H] with 12–17 points and six or more cards in the suit is competitive and does not invite game; [4H] with 18–21 points.
- After a jump raise of your major ([1H] – [3H]) and a pass, partner has at least 10 points: [4H] with 15–21 points (25 or more together), otherwise [P] with 12–14 points.

## Responses to [1NT]
- 0–7 points: [P], unless a transfer, weak Stayman or [2S] fits.
- [2D] shows five or more hearts, [2H] shows five or more spades (transfers), with any points. With five or more cards in both majors, transfer to the longer one; with equal length, to spades, but to hearts with five cards in each and 8–9 points. Opener bids the next suit, or jumps to three of it with 17 points and four or more cards (super-accept).
- After the transfer, responder bids [P] with 0–7 points; [2NT] with exactly five cards and 8–9 points; three of the major with six or more cards and 8–9 points; [3NT] with exactly five cards, no singleton or void, and 10–15 points; four of the major with six or more cards and 10–15 points. With 16 or more points, the guide gives no call.
- A new suit after the transfer is game forcing: exactly five cards in the major, four or more cards in the new suit, a singleton or void, and 10–15 points. With two such suits, bid the longer one, or the lower one with equal length. After [2D] [2H], [2S] instead of [2NT] shows five cards in each major and 8–9 points.
- After the transfer, opener answers [2NT] with 15 points by [P] with two cards in the major, or by three of the major with three or more cards; with 16–17 points, by [3NT] or four of the major in the same way. After three of the major, opener passes with 15 points and bids four of it with 16–17 points. After [3NT], opener passes with two cards in the major and bids four of it with three or more cards. After [2S], opener passes with 15 points and at least as many spades as hearts, otherwise bids [3H]; with 16–17 points, [4S] or [4H] in the same way.
- After the super-accept, responder passes with 0–5 points and bids four of the major with 6 or more points.
- [2C] (Stayman): 8 or more points with a four-card major. Opener answers [2D] without a four-card major, [2H] with four hearts, [2S] with four spades and not four hearts.
- Weak Stayman: [2C] also with 0–7 points, four spades, four hearts and at most one club. Pass any answer of opener.
- After [2C] [2D], responder bids [2H] or [2S] with five cards in that major, four cards in the other major and 8–9 points; [3H] or [3S] in the same way with 10–15 points, game forcing. Without a five-card major, [2NT] with 8–9 points or [3NT] with 10–15 points.
- After [2C] [2H], responder bids [3H] with four hearts and 8–9 points or [4H] with 10–15 points; without four hearts, [2NT] or [3NT] in the same way. After [2C] [2S], the same with spades. With 16 or more points, the guide gives no call.
- After Stayman, opener answers [2NT] with 15 points by [P] and with 16–17 points by [3NT]; after [2C] [2H] [2NT], with four spades, by [3S] or [4S] in the same way instead. After three of opener's major, [P] with 15 points or four of it with 16–17 points. After [3NT], [P], but [4S] with four spades after [2C] [2H].
- After [2C] [2D] and five cards in a major from responder, opener with two cards in that major bids [2NT] over a two-level bid with 15 points, otherwise [3NT]; with three or more cards, [P] over a two-level bid with 15 points, otherwise four of the major.
- [2S] (minor transfer): 0–7 points with six or more cards in a minor, or with five cards in a minor in a hand that is not 5-3-3-2. Opener must bid [3C]; pass with clubs, or bid [3D] with diamonds. With a five-card minor in a 5-3-3-2 hand, pass [1NT].
- [2NT]: 8–9 points without a four-card major, invitational. Opener passes with 15 points and bids [3NT] with 16–17 points.
- [3C] [3D]: 8–9 points with six or more cards in the minor, invites [3NT]. Opener answers as after [2NT].
- [3NT]: 10–15 points without a four-card major, to play. Opener passes.
- [3H] [3S]: 16 or more points with six or more cards in the major, slam interest. Opener bids four of the major with 15 points or with two cards in it; with 16–17 points and three or more cards, [4NT] (Blackwood).
- [4C] (Gerber): 16 or more points, an unbalanced hand without a four-card major; asks for aces. Opener answers [4D] 0 or 4, [4H] 1, [4S] 2, [4NT] 3. Then [5C] asks for kings in the same steps, from [5D]; any other bid is to play.
- [4NT]: 16–17 points, balanced, without a four-card major, invites [6NT]; not Blackwood. Opener passes with 15 points and bids [6NT] with 16–17 points.
- [6NT]: 18–21 points, balanced, without a four-card major. [7NT]: 22 or more points. Opener passes.
- The order: [3H] or [3S] first; with five cards in one major and four in the other, [2C] with 8 or more points, else the transfer; then the other transfers; then [2C]; [3C] or [3D] before [2NT].

## Responses to [2NT]
- [4C] (Gerber): 12 or more points, an unbalanced hand without a four-card major; asks for aces, as after [1NT]. The guide does not describe the other calls.

## Responses to [2C]
- [2D]: waiting, 0–7 points.
- [2H] [2S] [3C] [3D]: 8 or more points with a good five-card suit.
- [2NT]: 8 or more points, balanced hand.

## Responses to a weak two
- Raise partner's suit to make the auction harder for the opponents.
- [2NT]: 15 or more points, asks opener to describe the hand.

## Competitive bids
- An overcall at the one level: 8–16 points with a good five-card suit. At the two level: 10–16 points.
- [1NT] overcall: 15–18 points, balanced, with a stopper in the opponents' suit.
- [X] (takeout): 12 or more points, short in the opponents' suit, with support for the other suits; or any hand with 17 or more points.
- [XX] after partner's opening is doubled: 10 or more points.

## Targets for the partnership
- 25 points: game in [3NT], [4H] or [4S].
- 29 points: game in [5C] or [5D].
- 33 points: small slam.
- 37 points: grand slam.
- [4NT] (Blackwood) asks for aces: [5C] 0 or 4, [5D] 1, [5H] 2, [5S] 3.

## Names of the conventions
- Standard American is the usual natural five-card-major method in North America. SAYC (Standard American Yellow Card) is its common form for online play.
- Milton points: named after Milton Work, who made the count popular from about 1927.
- Weak two: Howard Schenken developed it in the early 1940s.
- Stayman: named after Sam Stayman, who published it in 1945. Jack Marx and George Rapée had the idea before him.
- Weak Stayman: a descriptive name; also called garbage Stayman.
- Jacoby transfers: named after Oswald Jacoby, who wrote about them in 1956. The minor transfer is the SAYC form.
- Super-accept: a descriptive name for opener's jump to three of the major after a transfer; it shows four-card support and a maximum.
- Blackwood: named after Easley Blackwood, who published it in 1933.
- Gerber: named after John Gerber, who brought it to North America in 1938.
